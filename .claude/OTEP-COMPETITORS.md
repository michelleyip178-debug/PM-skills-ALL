# OTEP Competitive Landscape — Internal Talent Marketplaces

Use this section when benchmarking OTEP, preparing for stakeholder conversations, or positioning OTEP's unique value internally.

## Commercial Platforms (External Benchmarks)

These are the platforms PSD likely benchmarked when designing OTEP. Use them to sharpen OTEP's differentiation.

| Platform | Strengths | Best known for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gloat** | AI matching across roles, projects, mentors, and learning | Most mature enterprise talent marketplace; used by Unilever, Mastercard | Complex deployment; requires significant data seeding to feel active |
| **Eightfold AI** | Infers skills from work history, not just declared profiles; covers mobility + hiring in one | AI depth and data completeness | Enterprise scale and cost; implementation complexity |
| **Fuel50** | Career pathing and personalised development; strong skills taxonomy | Best-in-class skill mapping and career mobility | Less strong on gig/project-based work vs Gloat |
| **Workday Talent Marketplace** | Fastest to deploy if already on Workday HCM; unified HR data | Integration with existing Workday ecosystem | Less granular skills matching than dedicated platforms |
| **SAP SuccessFactors** | Strong in large enterprises already on SAP; career and talent development modules | Enterprise HCM integration | Not a standalone talent marketplace; weaker on internal mobility depth |
| **Phenom** | Strong UX; explicitly serves government and public sector | AI-driven matching for government agencies; career visualisation | Broader talent acquisition focus; internal mobility is one part |
| **365Talents** | Skills-centric; good integration with Workday and SAP | European market; project-based work allocation | Less robust internal mobility vs Fuel50 and Gloat |
| **Beamery** | Combines internal HR data with external labour market intelligence | Workforce planning with market context | More planning-focused than employee-facing marketplace |
| **Cornerstone OnDemand** | Integrated LMS + talent marketplace | Learning and development ecosystem | Less AI-native than Gloat or Eightfold |
| **JobKred** | Singapore-built; AI skills matching designed for local market | Southeast Asia context; skills intelligence | Smaller ecosystem vs global platforms |

**Key differentiators to watch across all:** AI-inferred skills (vs self-declared), opportunity types covered (roles, gigs, projects, mentoring, learning), integration with existing HRIS, and employee adoption rates.

## Public Sector Equivalents (Government Benchmarks)

| Organisation | Platform / Initiative | What they did | Relevance to OTEP |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Singapore Public Service (PSD)** | One Talent Gateway (OTG) — OTEP's predecessor | Launched as pilot Aug 2022; AI-powered personalised recommendations for developmental opportunities and learning | OTEP is the next evolution of OTG — understand what OTG solved and what gaps remain |
| **US Federal Government** | USAJOBS + agency-level initiatives | Arizona Dept of Administration adopted internal talent marketplace for cross-training; focus on cybersecurity and cloud skills gaps | Shows government talent marketplaces work best tied to specific skill gap urgency |
| **UK Government** | Civil Service Jobs + internal mobility programmes | Cross-departmental secondments; Civil Service Fast Stream mobility | Less platform-driven; more policy-driven — useful contrast to OTEP's tech-first approach |
| **PwC** | My Marketplace | AI-powered internal marketplace for 360,000 employees; personalised dashboard for skills, opportunities, and career paths | Best private sector analogue for scale and user experience expectations |
| **Australia Public Service** | APS Mobility Framework + GovHerd | Cross-agency mobility; skills-based matching for surge capacity | Comparable WOG scope to Singapore's approach |

## OTEP's Unique Position

Unlike commercial platforms, OTEP is built specifically for the Singapore public service context:

- **WOG scope** — spans all agencies, not just one organisation
- **Public service constraints** — security, data governance, and policy compliance built in from the start
- **Career development + opportunities + learning in one** — the three-pillar model (Profile, Opportunities, Courses) mirrors what commercial platforms try to integrate but often treat separately
- **No commercial incentive** — designed for officer outcomes, not vendor revenue

When talking to Mark or Adrian, frame OTEP not as "our version of Gloat" but as a purpose-built WOG infrastructure that commercial platforms cannot replicate due to data sovereignty, cross-agency trust, and policy constraints.
