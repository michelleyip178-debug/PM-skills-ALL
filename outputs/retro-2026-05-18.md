## PM Growth Lens

---

### 1. Outcomes thinking vs. requirements

**What I did:**
The Sprint 2 reshuffle (May 14) was an outcomes move. Michelle didn't ask "which 5 stories do we have?" — she asked "does this sprint prove the end-to-end journey?" and answered no. Deferred OTEP-268 during grooming for the same reason: the empty state only matters when filters exist, and Sprint 2 always has data.

**Where I fell short:**
The "Up Next" list. 11 items as of Sprint 2 start, several sitting there since May 11. A PM who's thinking in outcomes would have fewer items and each one tied to "this blocks X from happening by Y." What the list actually looks like is a well-maintained backlog — complete, organised, and not prioritised.

Rethna's QA doc is the clearest example: it didn't have a hard outcome attached to it ("Sprint 1 can't close without QA confirmation") so it drifted.

**One thing to practise next week:**
Before adding anything to "Up Next," write the consequence of it not being done. If there's no clear consequence, it belongs in the parking lot.

---

### 2. Stakeholder influence

**What I did:**
The BO working agreement (May 11) was a genuine stakeholder influence move — setting rules of engagement before friction arose rather than managing BOs reactively. Nine decisions logged from the internal groom (May 13), including clear owner assignments on the open items that came out of it.

**Where I fell short:**
Open item #21 (Confluence/Jira BO visibility) was a commitment Michelle made at the BO working agreement. It hasn't been done. BOs were told they'd have async visibility into sprint goals and tickets. A week later, they don't. That undermines the working agreement she drove.

The apply flow flip also points to a gap: Michelle wasn't in the BO Senior Level meeting on May 12 where the Internal Jobs → FormSG decision was made. She caught it fast, but the question is whether she could have been in that conversation earlier.

**One thing to practise next week:**
Close the loop on the commitment made at the BO working agreement — #21 needs to be done before the next time she interacts with Jacky or Xian Zhang. Commitments made to stakeholders expire faster than internal tasks.

---

### 3. Roadmapping and prioritisation

**What I did:**
Real prioritisation calls this sprint: deferred OTEP-86/US-05 (filter polish) to Sprint 3 to make room for the detail page; removed OTEP-268 empty state from Sprint 2; clarified the apply flow cut-line (FormSG for three types, SJR deferred). These weren't obvious calls — each required articulating the tradeoff.

**Where I fell short:**
The design lock date has been an open item since May 13. Setting a design lock date is a low-effort, high-leverage prioritisation move — it prevents mid-sprint churn that no amount of grooming can recover from. Five days of not deciding is not a scheduling problem, it's a prioritisation problem. Michelle has Amber's design review tomorrow (Tue 19, 14:00). That's the moment to close it.

**One thing to practise next week:**
For every open item that has a day-specific ceremony or meeting this week, make a decision in that meeting. Don't carry it to the next. "Design lock date" gets closed tomorrow at the BO Design Review — not at the next standup, not at next week's grooming.
