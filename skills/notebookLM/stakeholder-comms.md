# Stakeholder Communication Prompts

Prompts for preparing stakeholder updates, meeting briefs, and alignment checks.

---

## Exec update for a specific person

> Write a 250-word update for [Mark/Jacky/Adrian]. Include: current state in 2-3 sentences, key decisions since last update, what's needed from them, and risks worth escalating. Cite sources.

---

## Pre-meeting brief

> I have a meeting with [name]. What's the latest context from the notebook? What open items involve them? What decisions need their input? Give me 3 questions to ask.

---

## Stakeholder alignment check

> Who are the key stakeholders mentioned across all documents? For each: what they care about, what they've been asked to decide, and what's still pending from them.

---

## Writing a one-pager

> Summarise the current state of [epic/feature] in a one-page format: problem, solution approach, current status, key risks, next steps. Keep it under 400 words.

---

## Steering committee prep

> Prepare a steering committee brief. Include: overall programme status (on track / at risk / off track), key milestones hit since last steering, decisions needed from steering, risks requiring escalation, and a 90-day outlook. Max 300 words. Use plain language — no jargon.

---

## Business owner update

> Draft an update for business owners [Jacky/Xian Zhang]. Focus on: what's been delivered that they can see or test, what requirements need their confirmation, what's changed since last discussion, and what they need to do before next sprint. Keep it conversational.

---

## Cross-team dependency request

> Draft a request to [team/person] about a dependency. Include: what we need from them, why (cite the spec or story), when we need it by, what happens if it's late, and a suggested next step. Keep it under 150 words.

---

## Status escalation

> Something is blocked or at risk. Draft an escalation for [Adrian/Mark]. Include: what's blocked, since when, what we've already tried, the impact if unresolved (which stories slip, which sprint is affected), and a specific ask. Direct tone — no softening.
