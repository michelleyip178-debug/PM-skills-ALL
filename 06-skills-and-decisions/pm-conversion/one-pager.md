## What I Do Now

I own the Opportunities epic for OTEP's MVP — the platform's core value proposition. That means I own the problem (officers can't find development opportunities without navigating multiple portals), the scope (20 user stories across 5 journey groups), and the delivery plan (5 sprints, May–July, with a Sep ship target).

I also own the WOG AD Authentication epic. Together, these two epics are the entire MVP.

## How I Operate Differently From a BA

### I frame work around outcomes, not requirements

The OTEP PRD doesn't start with features. It starts with a hypothesis: "If we provide a unified hub, officers will discover and apply in one place, leading to >= 50% channel migration from FormSG by Month 3." Every section ties back to that outcome. The success metrics have three tiers — outcome, input, and guardrail — each with specific event keys for measurement.

Sprint goals are written as what officers can do ("browse, filter, and scan all opportunity types"), not what the team delivers.

### I recommend, not present options

When the OTG and Careers@Gov filter taxonomies turned out to be incompatible, I didn't present three options and ask the team to pick. I researched all three, evaluated them against four criteria (officer mental model, technical complexity, MVP speed, future-proofing), and recommended Option C (hybrid model) with a clear rationale and a validation plan for four named stakeholders.

When scope pressure came up, I made five MVP-vs-R1 decisions in one session — each logged with date, rationale, and owner. I descoped competency matching, filter persistence, and the endorsement backend. Each had a reason tied to the MVP outcome, not "too hard."

### I sequence and prioritise, not just list

The sprint plan isn't a feature list split across sprints. It follows the dependency chain: auth first (gates everything), then data pipeline (gates UI), then hub (gates apply), then application routes (three independent streams parallelised). I identified that Sprint 3 has a cascade risk (OTEP-133 depends on two other stories) and flagged it before it became a problem.

I maintain a scoping gaps tracker with 13 items — each with an owner, a status, and a clear MVP-or-R1 decision. Three are formally deferred with decision dates. This isn't documentation for documentation's sake. It's the artifact I point at when someone asks "what about feature X?" and I need to explain why it's R1.
